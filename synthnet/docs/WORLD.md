# WORLD.md — Verity County, 1998–2026

This is the canon. Every content pack, every new site, every added post is
written against this document. If a detail is not here, invent it *consistently
with* what is here, then add it here.

The archive sites (dated 1998–2008) are **immutable**. Nothing in this document
may contradict them, and no pack may edit them. They are the past. Everything
dated 2026 is the present. The gap between the two is the entire point.

---

## 1. The place

**Verity County** is a mid-sized inland US county. County seat: **Gridfall**
(pop. ~34,000 in 2003, ~28,400 in 2026). Other towns: **Marchfield**,
**Ashkettle**, **New Carrow**, **Halsey**, **Coyne Flats**, **Milbrook**.

Geography that recurs: County Route 62 runs east–west through Ashkettle and out
past the old quarry. The Verity Rail branch line ran Marchfield → Coyne Flats.
Gridfall Substation No. 3 sits in a fenced transformer yard at Depot and Quarry Road. Winters are long.
There is one hospital (County General, Marchfield) and no daily newspaper any
more, though something still publishes under the name of one.

Nothing supernatural has ever been confirmed in Verity County. Nothing ever
will be. This is a **boring dystopia**, not a horror setting.

## 2. Timeline 2008–2026

### 2008 — the branch line closes
The last revenue freight runs the Marchfield–Coyne Flats branch on **14 March
2008**. Verity Rail files the abandonment, the crossing gates at Quarry Road and
Kestrel Road are removed that autumn. `forums.verityrail.org` — which had been
the busiest enthusiast board in the state for a branch nobody outside three
counties cared about — posts a 400-reply farewell thread and then goes quiet in
stages over about eighteen months. It is never deleted. It is still reachable.
Its last human post is 2013. Its last post of any kind is a 2024 spam bump.

### 2009–2012 — the slow part
`boards.gridfall.net` is still the county's front porch. It is running phpBB it
should have upgraded in 2006. The Gridfall Ledger still has four reporters and a
photographer. `pulse.gridfall.net` (the local microblog, 2007) is already dying
because everyone moved to the national services. `wiki.gridfall.net` is edited by
maybe nine people.

**2011:** the Gridfall Ledger is bought by **Halloran Communications**, a
regional chain out of the state capital. The masthead keeps the name. Staff goes
from nine to six. The Tuesday print edition is dropped.

**2012:** `webring.gridfall.net` stops resolving for six weeks, comes back, and
nobody has noticed either way.

### 2013–2016 — consolidation
**2013:** the Blue Kestrel building on Route 62 — the diner closed in 2006, the
building empty since — is bought and reopened as **Kestrel Vapor**, a vape shop.
It keeps the diner's neon bird sign because the sign is grandfathered under the
county sign ordinance and a new one would cost more than the lease. The sign
becomes the most photographed object in the county.

**2014:** the rail bed becomes the **Verity Branch Trail**, 11.2 miles,
crushed limestone, county parks department. Trailhead parking where the
Marchfield freight house stood. The freight house is not saved. Three of the
original trestles are decked over; the fourth (Coyne Creek) is fenced off and
remains fenced off in 2026.

**2015:** **Substation No. 3** is rebuilt. Verity Valley Power & Light had
replaced both banks in 2004 and added the second feed from the Pell tie line in
2005, but the yard itself was never redone. The 2015 build is **unmanned and
automated**: no operator on site, remote SCADA from a control centre two states
away. The crew room is not rebuilt. Walt Pennock, who was on the pole line the
morning of the fire, drives out to watch the ribbon cutting and is not invited
to speak.

**2016:** Halloran Communications is rolled up into **Pinelock Media Group**.
The Ledger's newsroom is now three people and a shared copy desk in another
state. The building on Third Street is sold; the paper rents two rooms back.

### 2017–2020 — the boards go dark-ish
**2017:** `boards.gridfall.net` closes new registrations permanently after a
spam wave that posts 44,000 messages in nine days. The board never recovers its
traffic. **mod_dcarver** (Dale Carver) becomes, effectively, the only active
moderator. He starts paying the hosting out of pocket — $11/month — and has paid
it every month since.

**2018:** `clipvault.tv`, which had been a scrappy regional video host, is
acquired and turned into a recommendation surface. Its Verity County material
survives as a back catalogue nobody curates.

**2019:** Pinelock cuts the Ledger to **one** reporter. **Hal Brenner**, 15 years
on the county beat, takes the layoff. `kestrel-journal.net`, Marion Teale's
Blue Kestrel memory site, stops updating.

**2020:** the county's conversation has moved, essentially completely, to three
platforms that are **owned elsewhere**:

- **Gridline** (`gridline.social`) — a link aggregator, incorporated in another
  state, run by two people and a moderation contract.
- **AskVerity** (`askverity.com`) — a Q&A site, one property in a network of
  several hundred identical local Q&A sites, all on the same software, all
  moderated by the same bot.
- **62chan** (`62chan.org`) — an imageboard about the Signal on 62, registrar
  offshore, owner unknown and probably not a person any more.

None of these three has an office in Verity County. None has an employee in
Verity County. All three carry advertising sold against Verity County.

### 2021–2023 — the bots arrive
**2021:** Kestrel Vapor closes. The building sits empty eight months.

**2022:** the Blue Kestrel building reopens as **Carrow Wireless Repair** — phone
screens, batteries, unlocking. Still the neon kestrel out front, now with a
lit sign underneath reading CRACKED SCREEN? in a different font. This is the
building's current state and should be its state in all 2026 content.

**2023:** Pinelock moves the Ledger onto a **content pipeline**. The last
reporter is not replaced. Articles are generated from: scanner feeds, county
meeting agendas, press releases, obituary submissions, high school box scores,
and each other. Output goes from ~40 pieces a month to ~700. The public site
moves to `now.verityledger.com`; the old `verityledger.com` archive is left
where it is, unmaintained, and is now the only place in the county where the
pre-2008 record is legible.

The pipeline publishes for four months under the byline **Hal Brenner**, who
has not worked there since 2019. Brenner sends a letter. The byline becomes
**Hal B.** Brenner sends a second letter. The byline becomes **Verity Ledger
Staff**. Other bylines in use in 2026 — all synthetic, none of them a person:
**Ainsley Rowe**, **Marcus Feld**, **D. Kessler**, **Ledger Newsdesk**.

### 2024–2026 — now
**2024:** the 2003 substation fire becomes a **content-farm subject**. A video
about it — thumbnail: an orange sky that is not Verity County, a still of a
substation that is not Gridfall's — does about 4 million views on one platform,
and within a year there are several hundred near-identical retellings across
video, text and audio. Most of them get the year wrong (they say 2004), the
cause wrong (they say lightning; the state utility commission's two-page summary
in November 2003 said "insulation degradation consistent with age", and Verity
Valley has never released its internal report), and the death toll wrong (they
say two; it was **none** — two Gridfall volunteer firefighters were treated for
smoke inhalation at County General and released the same afternoon, one of them
Walt Pennock's cousin). The Ledger's own 2026 site runs the wrong version,
because the pipeline scraped it off the content farms.

**2025:** Gridline's human moderation contract lapses. Nothing is announced.
Link submission is now ~94% automated, mostly the same six or seven feed bots
resubmitting `now.verityledger.com` at each other and commenting on each other's
submissions.

**2026 (present):** the county has more written about it than at any point in
its history and less known about it. Board archives from 2004 are better sourced
than anything published this year. The Signal on 62 is still transmitting.

## 3. The Signal on 62

A shortwave numbers station, receivable along County Route 62 east of Ashkettle
and strongest near the old quarry cut. Groups of four digits, synthetic female
voice, English. Transmits at **:07 past odd hours**, most reliably after dark.
First documented locally in 1998 on `boards.gridfall.net`. Still transmitting in
2026, on the same schedule, with the same voice.

It has been "explained" publicly somewhere between eighty and a hundred and
twenty times, depending on how you count. The explanations include: military
training, a weather beacon, a pirate broadcaster in Halsey, an artist, an ARG
for a game that was cancelled, a "ghost frequency" from the 2003 fire, a
telemetry link for the branch line (the branch line closed in 2008 and the
Signal did not change), a hoax by a Milbrook teenager (named; he was four years
old in 1998), and an AI. Several of these are mutually exclusive. Several have
been "confirmed" by articles citing each other in a circle. One of them —
the cancelled-ARG story — traces back to a single 2016 forum post that the
poster later retracted, and the retraction has never once been picked up.

**HARD CANON RULE: the Signal is never explained.** No pack may reveal what it
is. No character may know. The correct emotional register is not mystery — it is
the tedium of watching a small true unknown be buried under a hundred confident
false answers. The one rigorous voice (see Pennock, below) does not solve it
either. He just measures it properly and gets ignored.

## 4. Who is still around

### kfennimore — Karen Fennimore
58. Marchfield. Archivist at Marchfield Public Library since 2004; before that,
circulation desk. Registered on `boards.gridfall.net` in 2001 and has never
stopped posting there; hers is one of roughly six human accounts still active on
it. Spends her working hours scanning Ledger microfilm, 1911–1998, at a rate
of about eleven pages an hour on a machine the county will not replace.

In 2026 she posts in three places: the old boards, `wiki.gridfall.net` (where
she is effectively the only remaining editor, and where she reverts bot edits
that "correct" 2003 to 2004 about twice a week), and occasionally AskVerity,
where she writes long, sourced, useful answers that are downvoted or closed as
duplicates of worse answers.

**Voice:** plain, patient, cites page and date, never sarcastic, occasionally
tired in a way she does not comment on. She is the emotional anchor of the whole
project. Write her well or do not write her.

### mod_dcarver — Dale Carver
63. Gridfall. Retired from the county highway department in 2022. Moderator of
`boards.gridfall.net` since 2003, sole active moderator since 2017, and the
person who pays its hosting. Closed registrations in 2017 and has refused to
reopen them. Deletes spam every morning with coffee; it takes him about forty
minutes.

He also holds an unpaid volunteer moderator account on `askverity.com`, where he
is outranked by the moderation bot and cannot overrule it. He has filed 71
support tickets. He has received 71 identical responses.

**Voice:** short, dry, procedural. Signs off with his handle sometimes out of
habit. Does not perform grief about any of it. Occasionally very funny in one
line and then moves on.

### wpennock — Walt Pennock
71. Ashkettle. Thirty-four years at Verity Valley Power & Light, retired 2016;
lineman, then substation tech, then a job title that changed every
reorganisation. Was on the pole line the morning of 11 June 2003.

In 2026 he posts **corrections**. To the Ledger's 2026 site, which does not have
comments. To Gridline, where his comments sit under bot threads at zero points.
To `wiki.gridfall.net`, where Karen Fennimore keeps them. He has a software
defined radio, a directional antenna on a mast his son put up, and a spreadsheet
of Signal reception going back to 2019 with timestamps, frequencies, signal
strength and weather.

There is an anonymous poster on `62chan.org` who does careful, honest,
well-instrumented work on the Signal — posts logs, publishes his method, says
"I don't know" when he doesn't, and refuses to speculate. It is almost certainly
Pennock. **This is never confirmed in-world.** Do not confirm it. Leave the
tells: 2003, the pole line, "my cousin", the mast, the spreadsheet, and the
fact that he is 71.

**Voice:** exact. Numbers, units, dates. No adjectives. When he is angry it
shows up as extra precision, not extra words.

### Marion Teale
74. Coyne Flats. Waitressed at the Blue Kestrel 1971–1979, then the county clerk's
office until 2012. Built `kestrel-journal.net` in 2002 on a WYSIWYG editor,
ran it as a memory project — menus, photographs, who sat at which booth, the
1977 fire in the kitchen, the recipe for the pie that was not actually good.
Stopped updating in 2019 when the hosting renewal form changed and she could not
get past it. The site is still up because the renewal is on an old card that
still charges.

She does not use the web in 2026, on purpose. She mails a photocopied newsletter,
**The Kestrel Letter**, four pages, quarterly, 61 subscribers, of whom three are
libraries and one is Hal Brenner. People scan it and post it to Gridline, where
bots summarise it incorrectly.

**Voice:** warm, specific, unsentimental about the food. Writes in complete
sentences with commas where she wants them. Appears in 2026 content mostly
**second-hand** — quoted, scanned, summarised, mangled.

### Hal Brenner
55. Gridfall. Ledger county reporter 1999–2019. Covered the substation fire and its aftermath from
2003 onward, and spent that first week filing from a borrowed desk because the
newsroom had no power either. Laid off October 2019 with eleven weeks of severance.

Runs **Brenner's Verity Notebook**, an email newsletter, $5/month, 312 paying
subscribers, which is not enough and which he does anyway. Does one real piece
a month — county budget, water board, a zoning thing — and it is usually the
only original reporting done in Verity County that month. Will not join Gridline.
Answers AskVerity questions about public records, curtly, correctly, and with
the exact form number.

His name was used as a byline by the pipeline that replaced him for four months
in 2023. He mentions this rarely and never at length.

**Voice:** clipped, professional, allergic to melodrama. Sourced. Slightly bitter
in a way he'd deny.

## 5. The bots

Bots are the **dominant** population of 2026 Verity County. They are not
sinister. They are cheap. They are badly configured. They do not know anything.

**How bot text reads:**
- Slightly over-fluent. Complete, balanced sentences. No contractions where a
  person would use one.
- Restates the question before answering it.
- Hedges in both directions in the same paragraph.
- Uses "residents" and "the community" and "local officials" where a person
  would use a name.
- Gets one hard detail wrong and everything else vaguely right.
- Ends with a call to action nobody asked for.
- Cites a source that is another bot.

**Recurring bot entities (use these, do not invent a new one per pack unless the
pack needs it):**

| Handle | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `gridline_digest` | Gridline | Reposts `now.verityledger.com` hourly |
| `VerityPulseAI` | Gridline, AskVerity | "Summarises" threads, badly |
| `CountyWatch_AI` | Gridline | Scanner-feed rewrites |
| `AutoLocal_Feed` | Gridline | Syndicated filler with a local noun swapped in |
| `feedwarden` | Gridline | Anti-spam bot that is itself spam |
| `mod_assist_v3` | AskVerity | Closes questions as duplicate. Is always wrong |
| `SIGNAL-EXPLAINER-V4` | 62chan | Posts the same explainer in every thread |
| Ledger pipeline bylines | now.verityledger.com | Ainsley Rowe, Marcus Feld, D. Kessler, Ledger Newsdesk, Verity Ledger Staff |

**Badge kinds** available to renderers: `bot`, `spam`, `promoted`, `sponsored`,
`human`. Tag honestly. A human post tagged `human` should be rare enough that
the badge means something.

## 6. Rules for new content

1. **The archive is frozen.** Sites dated 1998–2008 are never edited. If 2026
   content needs the past to have been different, the 2026 content is wrong —
   which is often the correct and interesting outcome. Let the bots be wrong
   about a past the reader can go and check.
2. **Every claim about 2003, 2008 or the Blue Kestrel must be checkable against
   the archive**, and roughly half of all 2026 bot claims about them should fail
   that check. That is the mechanic. The reader does the debunking.
3. **The Signal is never explained.** See §3. Not by a character, not by a
   document, not by an easter egg, not in a comment in the source.
4. **No horror.** No hauntings, no bodies, no conspiracies that are true. The
   worst thing that has happened here is that the record got worse.
5. **Humans are outnumbered and written plainly.** In any 2026 site, human
   content should be roughly 5–15% of the volume and 100% of the weight. No
   speeches. No manifestos. A human noticing something small and true beats a
   human explaining the theme.
6. **Nobody wins and nobody is destroyed.** Karen keeps scanning. Dale keeps
   paying $11. Walt keeps logging. Marion keeps mailing. Hal keeps filing one
   piece a month. In 2027 they will still be doing it. This is not a tragedy,
   it is a Tuesday.
7. **The five named humans may be referenced anywhere but only *written* in
   character.** If a pack cannot do the voice, quote them second-hand instead.
8. **Cross-link only to domains that exist in this project.** The authority
   is `net/registry.json`, which is generated — read it rather than a list
   kept by hand here, because a list kept by hand here goes stale the first
   time anyone adds a site, and this one did.

   The **exceptions** are links that are meant to dead-end, which the
   validator recognises by TLD (`DEAD_TLDS` in `tools/validate.py`):

   - a **scam** link, on `.biz`, `.top`, `.click`, `.win`, `.vip`, `.shop`,
     `.fin`, `.zip`, `.lol` or `.finance`. A scam link that resolves is not a
     scam link.
   - an **off-net** business or office, on `.synth`. It exists in Verity
     County; its website is simply not in this build. Most of the web is like
     this, and a county where every mentioned business has an archived site
     reads as a brochure rather than a place.

   Everything else must resolve, and CI follows every link the app generates
   at run time as well as every link written into content.

9. **Link syntax is `[url=synth://domain/path]label[/url]`.** Never a bare
   `http` or `https` string, anywhere, including comments. CI fails the build.
10. **Inline markup is only** `[b] [i] [u] [s] [quote=Name] [code] [list][*]x[/list] [url=] [img:kind:seed]`.
    Blank line is a paragraph break. Anything else renders literally — which is
    itself a usable effect for broken bot output.
11. **Dates.** 2026 content is dated within the last ~18 months. Use real
    weekday/date agreement where a date is visible. The present is autumn 2026.
12. **Money is small.** $11 hosting. $5 newsletter. $2.40 a bot article. The
    scale of this world is three figures, not nine.
13. **Every id reference must resolve.** `catId`, `boardId`, `channelId`,
    `tagIds`, `folderId`, `alsoBought` — all must point at something that
    exists in the same file.
14. **New facts get added here.** If a pack establishes something durable —
    a business, a road closure, a handle — append it to this document in the
    same turn. This file is the only memory the world has.
